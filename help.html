TA & Host Attendance Tracking

How to track attendance in Google Meet as a TA (Teaching Assistant) or Co-Host

+

If you're a TA or co-host, you know the frustration: the professor or meeting organizer scheduled the Google Calendar invite, so you don't have host rights. Google's default attendance report only goes to the meeting creator, leaving you with zero data.

Attendance Tracker solves this completely. You don't need host permissions:

  1. Install the Add-on: Add Attendance Tracker from the Google Workspace Marketplace to your school or university Google account.
  2. Join the Call: Enter the Google Meet call as you normally do from the link your professor shared.
  3. Open the Side Panel: Click the Activities icon (the shapes/puzzle piece in the bottom right corner) and launch Attendance Tracker.
  4. Leave it open during class: The add-on logs who arrives, who drops early, and calculates total active minutes under your own authenticated session.
  5. Clean Sheet in Your Drive: When the class finishes, the attendance spreadsheet automatically saves directly into your own Google Drive in a folder named Meet Attendance Tracker. No need to ask the professor for a CSV.
Helpful Guides: Read our complete walkthrough for educators in Attendance Tracker for Teachers and Tracking Google Meet Attendance Without Being the Host.

Can I track attendance if I'm not the host or meeting organizer?

+

Yes. That was one of the biggest reasons we built this tool. Google's built-in attendance feature strictly emails the meeting creator—which is useless if a project manager, attendee, or co-host is the one responsible for attendance.

With Attendance Tracker, anyone physically present in the call who has the add-on open can generate a clean attendance report for their own records.

Note for Google Workspace domains: If your school or company enables domain-wide delegation (a quick 2-minute admin setup at attendancetracker.dev/setup), you will also see external and cross-domain attendees. On personal Gmail, you can track anyone sharing the call.

Do I need Google Workspace for Education Plus to take attendance?

+

No, absolutely not. Google locks its native attendance tracking behind their most expensive plans: Education Plus and the Teaching and Learning Upgrade. Most schools and districts can't justify paying hundreds or thousands of dollars in license fees just to see who showed up to class.

Attendance Tracker works across all Google tiers without extra upgrades:

  • Google Workspace Education Fundamentals (the free tier most schools use)
  • Google Workspace Education Standard
  • Google Workspace Business Starter, Standard, Plus & Enterprise
  • Personal free @gmail.com accounts

The Google Meet API itself isn't locked behind an expensive tier, so we pass that direct access on to you without the upgrade tax.

Does it work with a personal free @gmail.com account?

+

Yes! Just sign in with your regular @gmail.com address. If you're an independent tutor, fitness coach, freelancer, or running community meetups, you can track attendee join times, duration, and auto-export straight to Google Sheets for free. No Workspace organization account required.

Google Sheets Exports & Reports

How to export Google Meet logs to Google Sheets

+

You have two ways to export your attendance, both simple and fast:

  • Auto-Export (Hands-free): Just leave the side panel open while you teach or meet. When the meeting ends, the add-on automatically generates the spreadsheet in your Google Drive and emails you a summary with the direct link. You don't have to remember to click anything.
  • Manual Export (Mid-meeting): If you want an immediate snapshot right now (say, 15 minutes into a quiz or presentation), click the Sheet icon at the top of the side panel. It creates or updates the sheet on the spot.

Step-by-step breakdown: Export Google Meet Attendance to Google Sheets.

What data fields and columns are included in the Google Sheets export?

+

Each exported sheet tab is pre-formatted with clean fonts, frozen header rows, and formula-ready columns:

Column Header What It Tells You
Name Display name from the participant's Google Meet account
Email Email from the calendar invite or your Workspace directory
RSVP Status Accepted, Declined, Tentative, or No Response from Google Calendar
Late? Flags tardiness (e.g. +7m late) based on the scheduled start time
Join / Leave Time Exact timestamps of when they joined and when they departed
Duration (min) Total active minutes (accurately sums reconnections if WiFi dropped)
Attendance % Active duration divided by scheduled call length (capped at 100%)
Status Present, Left early, Absent, or Absent (excused)

Where are exported sheets saved, and who can access them?

+

Your sheets live directly inside your own Google Drive in a dedicated folder called Meet Attendance Tracker. Each recurring meeting becomes its own dated tab in a single workbook, keeping your Drive clean.

Privacy: Only you have access by default. We don't host your spreadsheets on our servers. You maintain full ownership and can use standard Google Drive sharing controls whenever you want to share a sheet with a professor, department head, or manager.

How to mark absent attendees as "Excused" before exporting

+

When you export a meeting synced with a Google Calendar event, Attendance Tracker compares joined participants against your invited guest list. For any invitee who didn't show up:

  1. Before finalizing the export, the calendar matching window shows all absent invitees.
  2. Check the Excused checkbox next to anyone who gave advance notice or had an excused absence.
  3. The spreadsheet logs them as Absent (excused) instead of an unexcused absence.
  4. This tag persists in the series database so multi-week calculations reflect the excused status.

Classroom, Recurring Series & Trends

How to track recurring classes and multi-week semester attendance trends

+

If your lecture, seminar, or sprint standup is scheduled as a repeating event on Google Calendar, Attendance Tracker groups all sessions under that series.

Visit your dashboard at attendancetracker.dev/history.html and click the Series tab to see:

  • Cumulative attendance across the entire semester (e.g. "Alex attended 26 of 28 classes (93%)")
  • Visual attendance percentages and attendance streaks
  • Automated drop alerts if someone who regularly attends suddenly misses 3 classes in a row

How to share attendance dashboards with administrators or parents

+

On the Series tab in history.html, click the Share button next to any series. This creates a secure, read-only link with a customizable 30-day expiration date.

Recipients can view student attendance records, trends, and dates without needing an account or installing the add-on.

Slack & Email Automated Summaries

How to send Google Meet attendance summaries directly to a Slack channel

+

You can post automatic attendance summaries directly into team Slack channels (like #eng-standup or #ta-room) in under a minute:

  1. Create an Incoming Webhook in your Slack workspace (api.slack.com/messaging/webhooks).
  2. In the Attendance Tracker side panel, click the Settings (gear) icon.
  3. Paste your Slack Incoming Webhook URL and save.
  4. Whenever a meeting ends or you export, a clean Block Kit summary card posts to your channel with present, late, and absent counts, plus a direct button to open the Google Sheet.

Troubleshooting & Setup

Why don't external participants show up? (Domain-Wide Delegation Setup)

+

Google Workspace security policy restricts participant queries to users within the organizer's domain unless domain delegation is enabled.

If you see a notice saying "Some participants may not appear", your Workspace administrator can enable service account delegation in under two minutes:

  1. Send your Google Workspace IT admin this link: attendancetracker.dev/setup.
  2. In the Google Admin Console (admin.google.com), they navigate to Security > Access and data control > API controls.
  3. They add our Client ID and the read-only Meet scope.

Once approved, external guests, parents, and cross-domain attendees will appear in your live roster.

Why does someone show as "Left" when they're still sitting in the meeting?

+

This is an honest quirk of Google's official Meet REST API: it has a 2 to 5 minute propagation delay when updating attendee states.

If an attendee's WiFi briefly drops or they refresh their browser tab, Google's API might briefly report their previous session as ended before broadcasting the new session ID. Don't worry—Attendance Tracker connects multiple sessions under the same participant and merges them upon export, so their total active duration and final attendance percentage stay accurate.

Does Attendance Tracker work on iPad or mobile devices?

+

To record attendance: You need to be on a desktop or laptop browser (Chrome, Edge, Safari, Firefox). Google Meet's native iOS and Android apps do not support side-panel add-ons yet (that is a Google platform limitation, not ours).

To view and manage reports: Everything works great on mobile! You can open your exported Google Sheets, view the history.html dashboard, and receive Slack/email alerts right from your phone or tablet. For tips, read Google Meet Attendance on iPad & Mobile.

Security, Privacy & Compliance

What permissions does Attendance Tracker request, and why?

+

We ask for the absolute bare minimum permissions needed to take attendance:

  • meetings.space.readonly — Reads who is currently in your Meet call and when they join or leave.
  • calendar.events.readonly — Reads matching calendar invitees to identify no-shows and calculate late arrivals.
  • drive.file — Creates and updates attendance spreadsheets in your Drive. Notice this is drive.file, not full Google Drive access—we can only view and edit spreadsheets created by Attendance Tracker, never your personal files, docs, or photos.

Does Attendance Tracker record meeting audio, video, or private chats?

+

Never. We literally cannot. The add-on does not have access to your camera, microphone, screen shares, or chat text. It purely collects roster metadata: names, arrival times, and departure times.

Is student and meeting data secure, private, and FERPA-compliant?

+

Yes. Data is encrypted in transit using TLS 1.3 and at rest using AES-256-GCM. We do not sell student or user data, and we never use your meeting records to train artificial intelligence models. Our architecture adheres to standard FERPA and COPPA privacy principles for educational institutions.
